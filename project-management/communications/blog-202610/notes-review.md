## Routes and themes across groups

Five themes recurred across rooms, to varying degrees:

**1. Accidental / opportunistic entry**
Explicitly named in Room 5 ("accidental stumbling into community role") and in the chat (the most-reacted participant message: "very random and opportunistic").
Room 1's speaker story also describes a non-linear journey through mentorship and networks rather than a planned career path.
Room 3's story refers to "recognition that people, knowledge sharing and community are an important part of any work" — a retrospective framing consistent with this theme.

**2. Love of facilitation and bringing people together**
Rooms 1, 3, 4, and 5 all surface this explicitly: "likes to facilitate conversations" (Room 4), "love working with people" (Room 5), "community…is an important part of any work" (Room 3).
Room 4 articulates it most precisely: "community managers care more about facilitating rather than their own research."

**3. In-betweenness / boundary-spanning**
Room 2 is the most articulate here: "An 'in-betweenness' seems to exist between actors in research communities"; work/capability gets lost at the boundary without professionalisation.
Room 6 frames this differently: the technical mindset was not the right frame; RCM provided "a better framework for proactively fostering that community."

**4. Professionalisation makes informal work visible**
Rooms 2, 4, and 6 all connect to this.
Room 2: "professionalisation helps keeping things from getting lost."
Room 4: people were doing community management informally then formalised when the community needed it.
Room 6: much of the work is "unfunded" and involves "voluntary labor" — having a framework helps.

**5. The community for community managers**
Room 5: "community for community manager allows not having to iterate what goes into it."
Room 6: "We've been looking for something like RCM for a while where we can have these conversations to avoid reinventing the wheel, share best practices."
This theme directly enacts both purposes: shared identity and legibility.

## How groups differed

Room 2 stood out: rather than immediately telling an origin story, the group grappled with whether they even identified as RCMs or had stories to tell.
This was the most reflective and questioning discussion, and produced the longest collaborative story — though it is more a set of open questions and framings than a draft narrative.

Room 6 was the most explicit about audience: naming RSEs and other RCMs, and raising a "goldilocks principle" about not leaning too heavily on either technical or non-technical framing.

Rooms 1 and 4 produced the most concrete individual stories; Room 1 alone recorded the reflection sub-tasks.

Rooms 3 and 5 have thinner notes but their collaborative stories are punchy and thematic rather than exploratory.

## How well was the purpose met?

**Shared identity:** strongly met.
The common themes — accidental entry, facilitation instinct, in-betweenness, professionalisation — emerged independently across multiple groups.
The warmth in the chat (opening greetings, thanks to the speaker) reinforces that the session built a sense of belonging.

**Legibility to outsiders:** partially met.
Room 2's "two equally valid stories for different audiences" question is the most direct engagement with legibility, but it remained open.
Room 6's collaborative story is the closest to a legible external statement: "We've been looking for something like RCM for a while where we can have these conversations to avoid reinventing the wheel."
The Turing Way Book Dash call to action in the chat (connecting the session's work to a public initiative) extended the legibility purpose beyond the session itself.

## Candidate takeaways

1. Many RCMs arrived at the role by accident or through networks, not by planning — and this is a shared, recognisable experience.
2. The in-betweenness of the work (boundary-spanning, boundary-maintaining) is both what makes it hard to describe and what makes it distinctive.
3. Having a community *for* community managers — a space to share practices and avoid reinventing the wheel — is itself part of the RCM Cooperative origin story.
4. Professionalisation matters not for prestige but to prevent work from being lost at the boundary.
5. The same story may need different framings for different audiences; the session surfaced the question without resolving it.
